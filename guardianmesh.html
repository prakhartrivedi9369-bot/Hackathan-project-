How the mesh responds

Three steps, running continuously

This is the actual order of operations GuardianMesh runs, from the moment ground conditions change to the moment resources move.

01 — Detect

Sensors read the ground truth

Seismic, flood, wind, and thermal sensors on the mesh report anomalies directly to neighboring nodes, no central server required.

02 — Triage

AI ranks what matters first

The model cross-references reports against population density and known infrastructure to rank incidents by urgency in seconds.

03 — Coordinate

Responders move on one shared map

Fire, medical, and utility teams see the same live picture and routing, even across agencies that never shared a system before.

What the mesh actually does

Every capability here exists because a past disaster response was slowed down without it.

Offline mesh comms

Nodes relay messages hop by hop across radio, satellite, and LoRa links when cell towers and internet are down.

AI damage assessment

Satellite and drone imagery gets scored for structural damage within minutes of a pass, no analyst queue required.

Automatic resource routing

The system suggests the nearest available unit for every incident, factoring in road status reported by the mesh itself.

Multi-agency sync

Fire, EMS, utilities, and shelters work from one incident record instead of five radios and a whiteboard.
